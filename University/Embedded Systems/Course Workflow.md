## Annotation and rendering conventions

- Slides remain the main material. Every page has one `## Slide N — title` section, one local PNG embed immediately below it, and a link to physical PDF page N. Title pages, repeated slides, references, and article footer pages are retained in order. Untitled article continuations have descriptive navigation headings. Differing printed slide numbers should be recorded separately when present.
- Comments are in English, in a `[!note] Comment` callout directly below the relevant page. Add only a specific missing explanation, small example, ambiguity, or evidenced correction; clear slides receive no commentary. Usually aim for 30–100 words when a comment is useful. Use LaTeX for mathematics, preserve lecturer terminology, and link to an existing explanation instead of repeating it.
- Keep exercise statements visible. Any added solution belongs in a separate collapsed `[!example]- Worked solution` callout. Do not require summaries, quizzes, or expanded textbook sections.
- Check the page and surrounding pages first. Consult primary external sources only where needed; cite them inside the relevant Comment. Apparent slide errors are explicitly qualified without changing the slide image. No lecturer remarks or exam requirements are inferred.
- PNGs were rendered locally with MuPDF (`mutool draw`), preserving the complete MediaBox and aspect ratio, with a 3200-pixel longest edge. Names are stable and zero-padded: `slide-001.png`, etc. No PDF-embed fallback was needed. MuPDF reported unavailable ICC support; the rendered examples were visually checked, but color-managed print fidelity is not certified. Original raster screenshot softness and content already clipped/covered in the PDFs remain visible.

## Continue on later runs

1. Read this workflow, the current index, every affected existing note, and the saved source map before editing. Preserve the user’s properties, comments, corrections, filenames, and any separate expanded notes. Check newly supplied syllabus or exam boundaries.
2. Inventory new standalone PDFs. Mirror their topic folder under `Notes/` and `Attachments/`, render the whole page, visually inspect every page, and use the same restrained annotation layout. Add finished notes to the index and mapping. Do not treat the optional archive as already processed.
3. For a revised PDF, save the previous source map and referenced images before rendering into a separate staging folder under `outputs/`. Compare the old and new page images and text, then inspect candidates visually. Match slide content across insertions, deletions, repetitions, and reordering; physical page numbers alone are insufficient.
4. Move a preserved comment only when its source slide is confidently matched. Flag comments whose slide changed or disappeared for review, retaining the old comment and source identity. Keep unmatched comments in a clearly marked review section rather than silently attaching them to another slide. Preserve user-written additions even when the generated baseline differs.
5. After review, update headings, page links, image mappings, and the saved fingerprints together. Repeat the coverage/link/image audit and record unresolved issues here. The import helpers are first-run utilities, not an automatic revision merger: `build_notes.py` skips existing notes, `prepare_slides.py` refuses to overwrite existing slide images, and `finish_course.py` refuses to replace an existing index/workflow.
