Source: [[University/Embedded Systems/Slides/3 Design of Hw Systems-20261026/Steps for IC manufacturing _ VLSI Tutorials _ Mepits.pdf#page=2|PDF page 2]]

> [!note] Comment
> Apparent process conflation: the “Epitaxial growth” paragraph correctly mentions growing crystalline silicon, then describes forming silicon dioxide. Epitaxy grows a crystalline semiconductor layer; thermal oxidation forms silicon dioxide from the silicon surface. These are distinct process steps. See [Applied Materials on epitaxy](https://www.appliedmaterials.com/us/en/semiconductor/products/processes/epitaxy.html) and [Colorado School of Mines on oxidation](https://sif.mines.edu/oxidation/).

## Slide 3 — Masking, etching, and doping

![[University/Embedded Systems/Attachments/3 Design of Hw Systems-20261026/Steps for IC manufacturing _ VLSI Tutorials _ Mepits/slide-003.png]]

Source: [[University/Embedded Systems/Slides/3 Design of Hw Systems-20261026/Steps for IC manufacturing _ VLSI Tutorials _ Mepits.pdf#page=3|PDF page 3]]

> [!note] Comment
> The mask specifies a pattern; etching removes exposed material; doping changes the electrical properties of selected semiconductor regions. These operations play different roles and are repeated as the structure is built. In particular, removing photoresist after pattern transfer does not remove the patterned device layer beneath it.
