Why competitive analysis?
FROM A PESSIMISTIC BOUND TO A MEANINGFUL COMPARISON
Worst-case and average-case answers do not tell the same story.
Worst case
An adversary can repeatedly request the current tail: 
Ω(mn) for any deterministic online strategy.
Average case
Requires a probabilistic assumption p(x); the access 
sequence might instead be bursty.
Competitive 
analysis
Compares online A with OPT on the same access 
sequence, even if OPT sees the future.
OPT is a mathematical benchmark, not a practical algorithm that can predict future requests.

--- PDF PAGE 11 ---
The move-to-front heuristic
Practice: Implementers discovered that the move-to-front 
(MTF) heuristic empirically yields good results.
IDEA: After accessing x, move x to the head of L using 
transposes:
exact cost = r + (r − 1) = 2r − 1 ≤ 2r
The MTF heuristic responds well to locality in the access 
sequence S.

Competitive analysis
Definition. An on-line algorithm A is 
-competitive if there exists a constant k such that for any sequence 
S of operations,
CA(S)    COPT(S) + k ,
where OPT is the optimal off-line algorithm (“God’s algorithm”).

LRU — online
Evict the page least recently requested. No future

--- PDF PAGE 37 ---
The competitive guarantee for LRU
SLEATOR–TARJAN (1985), THEOREM 6
Let LRU have k frames and offline MIN have h ≤ k frames.
F_LRU(S) ≤ [ k / (k − h + 1) ] · F_MIN(S) + h
If h = k:     F_LRU(S) ≤ k · F_OPT(S) + k
For k = 3 the factor is 3 (plus an additive startup term). In general this factor 
depends on memory size.
Reference: Sleator & Tarjan, “Amortized Efficiency…”, §4, Theorem 6, p. 207.

LRU in real systems
THE THEORY IS AN IDEALIZED MODEL
Linux memory management
Multi-Gen LRU groups pages by access recency to inform page reclamation.
docs.kernel.org/mm/multigen_lru.html
Redis in-memory cache
Approximated LRU samples eviction candidates rather than maintaining 
exact recency.
redis.io/docs/latest/develop/reference/eviction/
Important: real policies are variants/approximations; the theorem is about 
ideal LRU.

Takeaways
COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS CONNECTS THEORY AND SYSTEMS
1
Online algorithms cannot see the future
Evaluate them against an offline benchmark on the same request sequence.
2
Potential captures future benefit
Pairwise inversions yield a global proof even when single accesses are 
costly.
3
Recency also governs paging
LRU generalizes the idea, but needs a different proof and a k-dependent 
bound.
Question: why can LRU be costly on one sequence yet remain k-competitive?
